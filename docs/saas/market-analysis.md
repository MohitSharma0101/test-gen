# Sell the exam engine, not another Classplus

**Verdict: a conditional go.** Commercialise, but narrowly. Sell to offline JEE/NEET and school-board coaching centres in Rajasthan's tier-2 hubs, one institute at a time. Charge an annual fee of about ₹24k–₹60k + GST. Run three to five paid pilots *before* you build the super-admin console. A generic multi-institute "app for every coach" is a different business: there you would face Classplus, Graphy and Appx on their home ground, and the evidence says that business is hard even with venture money. Classplus's standalone SaaS line earned only **₹73.9 crore in FY24 with a ₹22.7 crore loss**, and the company now makes more money from its own test-prep content (Testbook) than from software ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/from-enabler-to-aggregator-classplus-flips-its-edtech-playbook/)). Teachmint grew mainly by selling classroom hardware and AI tools to schools ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/teachmints-revenue-jumps-43x-in-fy25-10550884)). The opening you have is the work these incumbents left behind. That work is the offline, print-first exam loop: chapter/marks-quota paper generation from the institute's own bank, printed student/answer-key/teacher copies, AI recheck of handwritten answer sheets, and measured weak-chapter insights, all tied to fees, attendance and results. None of the big white-label vendors researched sells that loop as one product. Small, disciplined Indian edtech SaaS can survive: Edmingle ran at ₹8 crore revenue with a ₹1.6 crore loss, and MyClassboard reached ₹25.9 crore and broke even ([Indian Startup Times](https://www.indianstartuptimes.com/?p=26350); [Indian Startup Times](https://www.indianstartuptimes.com/?p=26424)). The realistic goal for a solo founder is therefore a profitable regional business of roughly 50–150 institutes, not a unicorn. The biggest unknowns are willingness to pay and the support load per institute. Both can only be settled by talking to and piloting with real centres, and that is the first job.

> **How to read the numbers.** Figures with a link come from the research notes. Most were gathered through search-result extracts, because many primary pages could not be fetched. Anything marked *estimate* is my own arithmetic or judgement, and the assumptions are stated. Vendor prices are mostly third-party listings, because almost no vendor publishes an INR price list.

## Coaching spend is huge, but the software pool is small and crowded

Coaching itself is a large and resilient market. The NSO's Comprehensive Modular Survey: Education 2025 found that **about 27% of school students take private coaching**: 30.7% in urban areas and 25.5% in rural ones ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/27-of-students-rely-on-private-coaching-urban-participation-higher-cms-education-survey-2025/amp)). GST collected from coaching institutions rose from ₹2,240 crore in FY20 to **₹5,517 crore in FY24** ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/centres-gst-revenue-from-coaching-centres-jumped-nearly-150-in-5-years-at-rs-5500-cr-in-fy24/2204201)). At 18% GST, that implies roughly ₹30,000 crore of formal, GST-registered coaching turnover (*estimate*). Paid market-research estimates disagree with each other by an order of magnitude. Redseer projects test prep alone at **US$23–26 bn by FY30** ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/indias-entrance-test-preparation-market-to-reach-23-26-bn-by-fy30-redseer)). IMARC sizes coaching institutes at **US$7.2 bn in 2025** ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-coaching-institutes-market)). Treat both figures as rough direction, not precision.

The software slice is a different story. **No credible India figure exists for coaching-management or white-label app spend.** The best proxy is what the leaders actually earn, and the category leader's pure SaaS business is under ₹75 crore a year. A bottom-up sketch assumes 1–2 lakh centres able to pay ₹10k–₹50k a year. That gives **₹100–1,000 crore of total addressable software spend** (*estimate*), which is consistent with the vendors' size. The market can support a solo founder's business. It cannot easily support another venture-scale one.

Demand is moving in your direction geographically. Kota's enrolment fell from roughly 2–2.5 lakh to 85k–1 lakh as students dispersed to hometown hubs, before a reported 20–30% rebound in 2026 ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/drop-in-student-numbers-impact-kotas-coaching-hostel-industry/2393669); [ThePrint revival](https://theprint.in/india/kota-coaching-hub-sees-revival-admissions-rise-20-30-after-slump/2890969)). Sikar now reportedly hosts around 1 lakh aspirants ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/neet-ug-jee-coaching-hub-sikar-rajasthan-akola-maharashtra-patna-allen-kota-matrix-unacademy-physics-wallah-fees-hindi)). PhysicsWallah earned about half its FY25 revenue offline and is putting **₹460 crore of IPO money into offline and hybrid centres** in smaller cities ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/ipo-corner/story/ipo-bound-physicswallah-to-invest-rs-461-crore-in-offline-hybrid-centers-495714-2025-09-25)). This is double-edged. More independent mid-size institutes are appearing in tier-2 Rajasthan, which is your buyer. But national chains are arriving in the same towns, and some local centres will close. That pushes churn up, even as it gives the survivors a reason to run tighter testing and analytics.

Regulation adds a modest pull. Rajasthan's **Coaching Centres (Control and Regulation) Act, 2025** received assent on 23 Sep 2025 and comes into force on a date still to be notified ([PRS India](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/rajasthan/2025/Act16of2025RJ.pdf)). As reported, it requires registration above 100 students. It also bars lump-sum advance fees, requires refunds when a student leaves, and mandates counsellors and graduate tutors ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/rajasthan-passes-coaching-regulation-bill-2025-no-enrollment-below-16-mandatory-registration-for-institutes/amp)). Fee-instalment schedules, refund tracking, age and roll records and counselling logs are all things your software can hold. "Compliance-ready records" is therefore a credible pitch to centres above 100 students once the Act is in force. Note that centres below 100 students are exempt, so the pull is weakest exactly where the long tail sits. The research could not confirm whether the Act had been notified by November 2026.

**Demand evidence is the weakest part of this research.** There is no independent survey of coaching owners' pain points, no data on what share of small institutes use software rather than WhatsApp and Excel, and no first-hand reports of what they pay. The only paying-customer signal for AI features is Teachmint's FY25 revenue, where AI tools became the top line ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/teachmints-revenue-jumps-43x-as-ai-tools-become-top-moneymaker/tldr)). Customer interviews are not optional polish. They are the missing half of the market research.

The burn history matters more than the logos. Classplus spent **₹4 to earn every ₹1** of operating revenue in FY23 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=420045)). Teachmint spent **₹27 per rupee** in FY23 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=448330)). Classplus grew through quota-carrying sales teams in about 70 cities, with 70–80% of its customers in tier-2 cities and below ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2021/04/edtech-startup-classplus-pandemic-purplle-ivycap-ventures-nagarro)). That playbook was bought with venture capital, and it produced a SaaS line its own owner now ranks behind content. You cannot copy it, and you should not try. The more useful comparison is with the small, near-profitable vendors: Edmingle, MyClassboard and Testpress. They show that the niche pays founders who keep costs low and stay focused.

Customer pain with the incumbents is real but thinly documented. Small complaint samples against Classplus describe undisclosed charges for "basic features" after onboarding, repeated credit top-ups, WhatsApp (AiSensy) services sold as included and then billed, and refusals to refund ([Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/service-not-delivered/261712); [Voxya](https://voxya.com/consumer-complaints/misrepresentation-during-sale-deficiency-in-service-refund-denied/269627)). There are also disputes over transferring ownership of the app. Competitors use app ownership as a selling point: Testpress tells buyers to check whose Play account the app is published under ([Testpress](https://blog.testpress.in/best-appx-alternative/)). Keep the balance in view, though. Classplus's iOS app holds **4.4/5 from 7.2K ratings** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/classplus/id1324522260)), and the Voxya sample is only six complaints. The lesson is not that "Classplus is hated". It is that **transparent all-in pricing and an institute-owned app are cheap ways to stand out**.

## What you do differently is the offline exam loop, not AI

Lay the feature sets side by side. The big white-label vendors are built to *sell and deliver content online*: live classes, recorded courses, video DRM, storefronts and payments. The ERPs are built to *run admin*: attendance, fees and SMS. Your product sits between them. It does the work an offline centre does every week: set a paper from its own bank to chapter and marks quotas, print it in three versions, run the test, check the scripts, rank the students and find each student's weak chapters. The research found Proctur offering a question bank and MCQ auto-grading but no confirmed printable paper generator ([Proctur blog](https://proctur.com/blog/?p=527)). It found no vendor page confirming face-recognition attendance in any coaching platform. And it found handwritten AI checking only in separate point tools that have no batches, fees or attendance. Your **combination is not sold as one integrated product by any major vendor found** (sourced absence, with the caveat that Classplus, Testpress and Appx could not be fully checked for offline paper printing).

Be honest about which parts carry the value. "AI generates questions" on its own is being commoditised. Over 70% of Indian teachers already use AI tools, mainly for lesson planning ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/news-in-brief/70-of-indian-teachers-using-ai-in-classrooms-centa-survey-reveals/)). Google made 30+ Gemini tools free in Classroom for education editions ([Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2025/06/gemini-google-classroom-all-edu-editions.html)). That free route is officially closed to coaching businesses: Google's eligibility rules exclude "organizations offering mentoring or coaching" and "examination companies" ([Google Workspace](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/getting-started/editions/qualifications-for-google-workspace-for-education)). Even so, a teacher can paste a chapter into free ChatGPT. The defensible value is in the workflow: the institute's own vetted, Mathpix-clean math bank, quota-based generation, print-ready output, results saved to each student, and **batch-scale checking of handwritten scripts**, which no free tool does. Low AI literacy also helps you. Only 57% of teachers answered basic AI questions correctly in the CENTA survey, which favours AI hidden inside a simple tool over a raw chatbot.

Your other differentiators are modest but real. Face-recognition attendance on a phone camera, with no hardware, is a genuine gap: no free coaching app was found, and paid options are priced per employee at ₹25–₹180 per user per month ([Capterra India](https://www.capterra.in/software/1107394/iLoggedin-Face-Attendance-App); [WappBlaster](https://wappblaster.com/products/attendance-management-app/)). It is also your largest legal liability (see the architecture section). The student PWA offers ranks, leaderboards and chapter insights, which sits at parity with the incumbents' branded apps. The PWA format, however, avoids store fees and template-app policy problems. The gaps cut the other way too. You have no live classes, no video hosting or DRM, no course storefront, no online fee payment links (not confirmed in your feature list), no OMR bubble-sheet scanning and no parent app. For an offline centre those are acceptable omissions. For an online creator they rule you out.

## Free and cheap alternatives cap the price for small centres only

There is **no free all-in-one option for an Indian coaching centre**. The practical "free stack" is WhatsApp or Telegram groups, consumer Google Forms and Sheets, and capped freemium tools. Wayground (formerly Quizizz) made its Basic plan free in 2026 ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/quizizz/diffs/2026Q1)). ZipGrade scans 100 OMR sheets a month free, then charges **$6.99 a year** for unlimited scans ([ZipGrade](https://zipgrade.com/pricing/)), which makes OMR alone a commodity. Zoho Classes advertises a free edition for up to 100 students (snippet only, [Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/en-in/classes/pricing/)). Teachmint's free tier is contradictory across sources ([fitgap](https://us.fitgap.com/products/021746/teachmint); [xpay](https://www.xpay.sh/saas-pricing/teachmint/)).

Open source does not solve it either. Fedena's open-source repository has had **no commits since July 2016** ([GitHub](https://github.com/projectfedena/fedena/commits/master)). Gibbon and OpenEduCat are maintained but built around schools ([gibbonedu.org](https://gibbonedu.org/download/); [GitHub](https://github.com/openeducat/openeducat_erp)). MoodleCloud starts around $160–170 a year for 50 users ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/moodle/diffs/2026W43)). None of them handles batches, fee instalments or quota-based JEE/NEET paper printing out of the box, and all of them need a technical person. Vendors themselves report that the owner's real objection is "ERP fees versus free Google Sheets", together with staff fatigue from complex software ([Classpro blog](https://blog.classpro.in/?p=3295)). Your simplicity-first design answers the second objection. The first is answered only by showing time saved on papers and checking, or money recovered on fees.

## Price at ₹24k–₹60k a year and undercut the stack, not Classplus

The market has two price models. **Branded-app and LMS vendors** charge a flat annual fee. Classplus is listed at **₹19,999 to ₹31,999 a year + GST** across its tiers ([Capterra India](https://www.capterra.in/software/208852/class-plus)). Graphy's Launch plan is **₹24,999 a year plus a 10% fee on each sale** ([Graphy support](https://graphy804657060717855613.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/1060000123255-what-are-the-different-pricing-plans-available-on-graphy-and-how-do-i-choose-the-best-one-)), and its upper tiers are listed at ₹2–5 lakh a year ([SaaSWorthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/graphy-software/pricing)). Testpress is listed from ₹2,699 a month ([Techjockey](https://www.techjockey.com/compare/testpress-vs-testbook)). **Coaching ERPs** charge per student. EduSys cites **₹12–₹20 per student per month** as the going rate ([EduSys](https://edusys.co/en-in/coaching-center-management-software.html)). Class Matrix charges ₹100 per student per year with a 200-student minimum ([Class Matrix](https://classmatrix.org/pricing.php)).

The point tools that overlap with your features add up. Paper generators run ₹500–₹10,000 a year ([Examin8](https://examin8.com/blog/?p=395); [Techjockey](https://techjockey.com/detail/megaexams)). OMR software costs ₹5k–₹20k ([Capterra India](https://www.capterra.in/software/1096928/Alpha-OMR)). AI answer checking is about **₹2 per page** at ClassPulse ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1242133/ClassPulse)) and is claimed at ₹0.40 per page by Parakh ([Parakh](https://www.parakh-ai.com/)). Bought separately, a 200-student centre's stack of branded app, ERP, paper generator and OMR costs roughly **₹45k–₹90k a year** before usage charges (*estimate* built from the listed prices).

That sets your pricing. Anchor just above Classplus's entry tier, justify the premium with the exam loop, and stay below the cost of the stack.

| Plan (*recommendation*) | Who | Price (+18% GST) | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | ≤100 students, one branch | ₹18,000/yr | Staff app, branded student PWA, question bank + paper generation/print, manual attendance, fees, results, online tests; small AI quota |
| **Standard** (lead plan) | 101–400 students | ₹36,000/yr | Everything, plus face attendance, AI answer-sheet recheck (e.g. 2,000 pages/month), batch and student insights, compliance-ready fee/refund records |
| **Institute** | 400–1,000 students or branches | ₹60,000/yr | Higher AI quota, multi-branch, priority support; optional branded Play Store app published under the institute's own account |

| WhatsApp wallet | All | Prepaid, at Meta rate + margin | Utility ~₹0.12 and marketing ~₹0.86 per message + GST ([Monty Mobile](https://montymobile.com/blogs/whatsapp-business-api-pricing-in-india-inr-rates-gst-and-the-2026-currency-migration-deadline)) |
| AI overage | All | ~₹0.50–₹1 per page | Undercuts ClassPulse's ₹2/page |

Bill annually and upfront, with a discount for doing so. Publish the full price, including the WhatsApp and AI rules, on one page. "No hidden charges, your app is yours" is a direct answer to the incumbents' complaint record. Give founding pilot centres 40–50% off the first year in exchange for a reference and a testimonial video, not a free plan. Free pilots do not test willingness to pay. These prices are *recommendations* built from listed competitor prices. The research found no first-hand data on what small Rajasthan centres actually pay, so test them in interviews and adjust.

At ₹36k a year on Standard, that is a **gross margin of roughly 75–90%** (*estimate*). Payment-gateway fees and any rise in Gemini prices would trim it a little. Infrastructure is not where the business fails.

The constraint is your own time. The notes estimate **1–2 hours of support per institute per month** in the early period for non-technical teachers on phones (*estimate*). At 50 institutes that is 50–100 hours a month, nearly a part-time job before any sales, onboarding or development. Churn is the second drag. No Indian SMB SaaS churn benchmark exists ([ProductGrowth.in](https://productgrowth.in/numbers/b2b-saas-churn-rate-india/)). Global SMB figures of 3–7% a month ([Tenet](https://www.wearetenet.com/blog/saas-churn-rate-statistics)), plus centres closing as chains move in, suggest planning for **25–40% annual logo churn** (*assumption*). Under those assumptions, the revenue scenarios look like this. Twenty institutes at an average of ₹36k gives about **₹7.2 lakh a year**, which validates the idea but does not replace a salary. Fifty institutes at ₹40k gives **₹20 lakh**, a solid solo income after costs. A hundred gives **₹40 lakh**, which is only reachable with self-serve onboarding, a support hire or a reseller. Holding 50 institutes at 30% churn means signing about 15 new ones every year just to stand still. **Retention and onboarding efficiency decide the outcome, not price or infrastructure.**

## Segment fit: lead with JEE/NEET centres, skip YouTubers

**Offline JEE/NEET and school-board coaching, 100–800 students, tier-2 Rajasthan: strong fit, sell here first.** Everything you built serves this buyer: Mathpix math, chapter-quota papers, printed copies, handwritten scripts, ranks and weak chapters. They are above the Rajasthan registration threshold, so the compliance pitch applies. They often charge enough per student to afford ₹36k a year. Ajmer is your home and your first reference, and Sikar, Jaipur and Kota are within reach. Competition here comes from Proctur, generic ERPs and Classplus's branded app, and none of them leads with the exam loop.

**Competitive-exam (government job) coaching: medium fit, second wave.** Batches are large and the work is MCQ-heavy and often in Hindi. Volume favours OMR-style checking over handwritten AI recheck, and a Hindi bank for general studies and reasoning matters more than Mathpix math. Many of these institutes also sell test series online, which is Classplus/Testbook territory, including Classplus's "Samadhan AI" for government-exam students ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-in/aifirstmovers/classplus)). Attendance, fees, results with rank, and printed papers still apply. Enter once Hindi content and bulk MCQ checking are solid.

**Small tuition centres under 100 students: weak fit, serve only with Starter.** They are exempt from Rajasthan registration, closest to the free WhatsApp-and-Sheets stack and most price-sensitive. Each one costs nearly as much support as a 300-student centre while paying half as much. Take them only through self-serve signup once onboarding no longer needs you. Do not sell to them door to door.

**Online coaches and YouTubers: no fit, do not target.** This is the core market of Classplus, Graphy and Appx. Their products centre on course storefronts, video DRM, live classes, payments and commission-based monetisation. Graphy alone takes 3–10% of sales across tiers ([SaaSWorthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/graphy-software/pricing)). You have none of those features, and building them would turn you into a fourth undifferentiated content platform. Treat creators who also run an offline centre as JEE/NEET or competitive-exam customers.

## One multi-tenant backend, PWA first, institute-owned store apps

**Build one shared multi-tenant deployment, not an app per institute.** Put a `tenantId` on every document, store per-tenant branding and config, and enforce tenant scoping in one query layer or middleware rather than in each route. A scoping bug that leaks one institute's students to another is the defining risk of this design, so test isolation explicitly. Separate deployments per institute make isolation easy, but costs and maintenance grow with every customer, which is unrealistic for one developer beyond about 10 tenants (*judgement*). There is a middle path: a shared app with a database per tenant on one Atlas cluster. It makes contract-end export and deletion simple at modest overhead, and is worth considering given DPDP deletion duties. Note that adding `tenantId` to every schema is a database change, so plan the migration deliberately. The super-admin console only needs four things at first: create a tenant, set its plan and quotas, view usage, and suspend or export. Billing can stay manual (invoice plus UPI) until you pass about 20 tenants.

**Ship a branded PWA per institute on a subdomain.** Use the institute's logo, colours and manifest. A PWA avoids store review and fees entirely ([QuickCoach](https://www.quickcoach.fit/branded-coaching-app-cost.html)). For institutes that insist on a store listing, publish one container app where students enter an institute code. Offer separately branded store apps only as a paid upsell, published under the **institute's own developer account**, which costs them a one-time $25 on Google Play ([Google](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13306652)). This follows Google's February 2025 advice to white-label developers to use a decentralised account set-up ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15931448?hl=en)). Google's spam policy bans "multiple apps with highly similar content" built from templates, and violations can suspend the whole developer account. That would take down every app on it ([MonetizeMore](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/avoid-repetitive-content-violation/)). Apple's guideline 4.2.6 rejects template apps unless the client submits them, and Apple tightened 4.3 in June 2026 ([AppInstitute](https://appinstitute.com/apple-app-store-guidelines/); [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/06/09/app-store-guidelines-low-quality-apps/)).

**DPDP needs work before you scale, especially for face data.** The DPDP Rules were notified on 14 Nov 2025, and most obligations, including Rule 10 on children's data, bind from about **May 2027** ([India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/dpdp-rules-2025-india-data-protection-law-compliance-40769.html)). Under the law, the institute is the Data Fiduciary and you are its Data Processor ([SS Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-childrens-data-are-you-compliant/)). Processing data of anyone under 18 needs verifiable parental consent. Educational institutions get an exemption for monitoring done "in the interests of safety of students" ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/privacy-protection/1709084/8-key-takeaways-the-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025)), but **whether that covers face-recognition attendance is unresolved**, and a lawyer should answer it before face attendance is sold widely. Penalties for breaching children's-data obligations reach ₹200 crore (secondary summary, [BDO India](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/news/2025/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-%E2%80%94-check-provisions,-implementation-plan,-penalties)). The practical baseline is:

- Sign a data-processing agreement with each institute.
- Capture parental consent by OTP to the parent's phone and keep the record.
- Make face attendance opt-in, with manual attendance as the fallback.
- Store face embeddings only, never raw photos, and delete them when a student leaves.
- Give each institute an export and delete function.
- Disclose that answer sheets go to Gemini, which is a cross-border transfer.

## The risks that would kill this, ranked

The **top risk is founder bandwidth**. Selling, onboarding, support and development all compete for one person, and support grows with every institute added. Mitigations: a paid onboarding fee, a self-serve setup checklist, short Hindi how-to videos, and a cap on new tenants per month.

The **second is weak willingness to pay**. Indian SMB software leaders lose money even at low prices: Vyapar spent ₹2.04 per rupee of revenue in FY25 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/vyapar-posts-rs-63-cr-loss-in-fy25-cash-reserve-fades-93-10819211)). Classplus's SaaS line flattened despite heavy spending. If interviews show owners will only pay ₹10k–₹15k, the business cannot pay a solo founder at realistic tenant counts.

**Third is AI commoditisation and model risk.** Question generation is already free elsewhere, and Gemini's prices and models change. Keep a provider abstraction and per-tenant quotas. Validate recheck accuracy on real scripts: competitors' 95–98% accuracy claims are self-reported ([Parakh](https://www.parakh-ai.com/); [DASES](https://www.dasesai.com/)), and Chanakya AI's own FAQ concedes that handwriting and scan quality limit results ([Chanakya AI](https://aichanakya.in/product/checking)).

**Fourth is legal exposure**: DPDP for minors and biometrics, and store policy for branded apps. Both are manageable if designed in now.

**Fifth is concentration.** Early revenue from a handful of Ajmer centres is fragile against closures and chain competition. Spreading through Sikar, Jaipur and Kota, and through a reseller partner, reduces it.

**Finally, incumbent response.** Classplus could bundle print-paper features. But its strategy now points toward content, so this is a slower threat than it looks.

## A 90-day plan that earns the right to build the super-admin

Do **not** build the super-admin console first. Run the first pilots as tenants you set up by hand with a script on a minimal `tenantId` layer. Build the console only once paying centres prove the demand.

| Phase | What to do | What it must validate (go/no-go gate) |
|---|---|---|
| **Weeks 1–3: interviews** | Talk to 15–20 owners of 100–800-student centres in Ajmer, then Sikar, Jaipur and Kota, plus 3–5 government-exam centres. Show your Ajmer centre's real printed papers and insights. Ask what they use today, what they pay, who sets papers, how long checking takes, and how fees are chased. | At least 5 owners name paper-setting/checking or fee recovery as a top-3 pain. At least 3 say they would pay ₹24k+ upfront. Learn whether a PWA is acceptable or a store app is demanded. |
| **Weeks 3–6: paid pilots** | Sign 3–5 centres at the founding price (e.g. ₹18k–₹20k for year one, annual upfront). Onboard them yourself; time every hour spent. | At least 3 pay. Onboarding takes ≤6 hours per centre, including bank import. Each centre prints at least one auto-generated paper per week. |
| **Weeks 6–12: measure** | Track weekly active teachers, papers printed, scripts rechecked, fee dues recovered, and support messages. Test AI recheck against teacher marks on 200+ real scripts. | Support ≤2 hours per centre per month by week 12. Recheck agrees with teachers closely enough that they trust it (set the bar with them). At least 4 of 5 say they would renew at list price. |
| **After the gates pass** | Build the super-admin console (tenants, plans, quotas, usage, suspend/export), DPA and consent flows, and a legal opinion on face data. Then sell by referral in Rajasthan and approach one coaching association or book publisher as a reseller at 20–30% revenue share. | — |

**Kill or pivot signals:** fewer than 3 of 20 owners willing to pay ₹20k+ upfront, support stuck above 4 hours per centre per month, or pilots that use only attendance and fees. In that last case you are a commodity ERP and should either price as one or stay a single-institute tool. If owners value only the paper generator and recheck, a narrower, cheaper "exam engine" product sold to many centres and teachers could beat the full ERP.

The go-to-market should play to a solo founder's strengths, not imitate a sales team. Use in-person, founder-led demos with your Ajmer centre as the live proof. Follow up and support over WhatsApp, which Indian SMBs expect ([Mewayz](https://mewayz.com/blog/how-indian-smbs-buy-software-2026)). Post short Hindi demo videos showing a paper being generated and printed in two minutes. Offer a referral credit to each centre that brings another. Time the compliance-records pitch to the Rajasthan Act's notification. Stay within a 3–4-hour radius of Ajmer for the first 30 customers.

## Where this research is thin

Several findings rest on missing evidence and should be treated as open questions:

- **Number of centres.** There is no official count of coaching centres, nationally or for Rajasthan, and no size breakdown.
- **Buyer behaviour.** There is no first-hand data on what small centres pay, how long they take to buy, or how often they churn. No CAC or retention figures exist for any competitor.
- **Owner pain points.** No independent survey of coaching owners exists. The YouTube research tool failed to connect, so comment-based voice-of-customer work was not done.
- **Competitor financials.** Classplus's FY25 figures were not found. Appx, Proctur and Testpress prices are unpublished.
- **Competitor features.** Whether Classplus, Testpress or Appx offer printable paper generation was not confirmed.
- **Rajasthan Act.** Whether it has been notified, and its exact record-keeping clauses, were not verified.
- **DPDP and face attendance.** The DPDP penalty schedule comes from a secondary summary. How the Fourth Schedule exemption applies to face attendance is a legal question no source settled.
- **Running costs.** Gemini token counts per handwritten page are estimates, and Cloudinary pricing was not retrieved.

The customer interviews above are the cheapest way to close most of these gaps.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "can I beat Classplus?" to "will enough offline centres pay for a better exam week?". The incumbents' own numbers show that a generic coaching app does not make money even with hundreds of crores behind it. The money went into content (Classplus) and classroom hardware (Teachmint), which leaves the operational and assessment core of offline coaching underserved. Your product is already built around that core, and the costs of running it for each extra institute are trivial. What is unproven is demand, and that can be tested in about 90 days for almost no money.

The most important strategic choice is restraint. Stay offline, print-first and regional. Make transparency and ownership part of the offer. Spend your scarce hours on onboarding, not on a super-admin console nobody has paid for yet. If the gates pass, you are building a durable, high-margin, 50–150-institute business. If they fail, you will have learned cheaply that the asset is a good tool for one institute, or an exam engine worth selling on its own, not a platform.
